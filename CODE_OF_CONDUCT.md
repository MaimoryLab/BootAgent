# BootAgent Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

BootAgent is committed to a respectful, inclusive, and technically constructive
community. We welcome participation regardless of age, background, body size,
disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, level of experience,
nationality, race, religion, sex characteristics, sexual identity or orientation,
or socioeconomic status. Everyone participating in the project is expected to
help make collaboration safe and productive.

## Expected behavior

Community members should:

- discuss ideas and code on their merits, without attacking the person presenting
  them;
- assume good intent while still asking for evidence, tests, and clear reasoning;
- give specific, actionable feedback and accept reasonable correction;
- respect different levels of experience and explain project-specific context;
- keep issues, pull requests, and discussions focused on BootAgent;
- protect other people's privacy and redact credentials, personal data, and private
  communications; and
- stop behavior that another participant or a maintainer identifies as harmful.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following behavior is not acceptable:

- harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking, or sustained disruption;
- discriminatory, sexualized, insulting, or demeaning language or imagery;
- personal attacks, deliberate misgendering, or unwanted attention;
- publishing another person's private information without explicit permission;
- sharing API keys, access tokens, vulnerability details, or other secrets in public;
- spam, impersonation, deceptive links, or coordinated manipulation;
- pressuring someone to continue a conversation after they have asked to stop; or
- retaliation against someone who raises a concern or participates in an
  investigation.

Good-faith technical disagreement is welcome. Repeated hostility, badgering, or
turning a review into a personal dispute is not.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all BootAgent project spaces, including issues,
pull requests, discussions, reviews, release communication, and other public or
private channels used to represent the project. It also applies when someone is
acting as a project representative elsewhere.

## Reporting a conduct concern

For conduct involving content in this repository, use GitHub's **Report content**
action on the relevant issue, pull request, discussion, comment, or other item and
send the report to the repository administrators. Reports are handled privately.
Include links, dates, and a concise description when available, but do not collect
or republish sensitive material merely to strengthen a report.

If the concern is not attached to reportable GitHub content, contact a repository
administrator through GitHub and request a private reporting channel. Do not post
sensitive details publicly.

Security vulnerabilities are handled separately. Follow `SECURITY.md` rather than
using a conduct report or a public issue.

## Enforcement

Repository administrators are responsible for interpreting and enforcing this
policy. They may remove or edit content, close or lock conversations, issue a
warning, temporarily restrict participation, or permanently ban an account. The
response will be proportionate to the behavior, its impact, prior incidents, and
whether the person cooperates in stopping the harm.

Administrators will limit access to reports to people who need the information,
avoid unnecessary disclosure, and recuse themselves when they are directly involved
in the reported incident. Knowingly false reports or retaliation may themselves be
treated as violations. A person affected by an enforcement decision may ask an
uninvolved repository administrator to review it.
